Counterintuitive rulings worth memorizing

  • Respawn sets Shield to a flat 20 — not your Body Shield's limit. Rare/Epic/Legendary shields return under-charged. p. 31
  • Equipping a shield only fills you via its restore bonus. An Epic shield leaves you at 30/40 (not 40), because equipping restores 10 from your starting 20.
  • Health is hard-capped at 40, permanently. Only the Shield side of your HP pool can grow.
  • You can never be pushed off the Map — it's a Collision (stop at edge + extra damage). No ring-out kills.
  • Optics don't extend range — they only lower To-Hit Difficulty within the optic's own range band.
  • Thermite doesn't linger — one 15-damage hit, then the token is removed (no wall of fire).
  • A dead Legend's already-placed timed abilities still trigger on schedule.
  • Cover costs no MP (the Obstacle tag does), and Impassable never blocks grenades.

Sec 1 Setup & Components

How do you pick your Legend's starting gear?

You don't — there's no draft. Every Legend automatically begins fully equipped with the three Common gear tokens (Helmet, Body Shield, Knockdown Shield) placed in their Gear slots during setup, with the dial set to Shield 20 / Health 40. These Commons are deliberately set aside from the Common Loot bag and handed out directly. All three Common gears have no special effect.

Actual gear choices happen later, in Stage 1: Legend Loadout, where you may equip better tokens you draw — holding only 1 of each gear type at a time (drop the old one to swap).

pp. 8, 13

What is a Legend board?

Your personal player mat (one per Legend) that organizes everything you carry. It has five areas: Gear slots (Helmet / Body / Knockdown, 1 each) · 8 Backpack slots (Attachments, Grenades, Consumables — 1 token per slot) · Ammo tracks (0–5 per weapon) · 2 Weapon card slots (max 2 weapons) · Feat slots (deck + discard). The Player screen sits on it to hide your Backpack from opponents.

Don't confuse it with the Legend info sheet (your ability reference) or the Equipment board (a separate component with a Boss-mode side).

pp. 11–13

What are Lifeline's 13 loot tokens?

A loot set unique to Lifeline, tied to her Care Package ability and drawn from a separate Care Package Loot bag (not the general pools). Rarity breakdown:

RarityCount
Rare6

Notably 3 Legendary tokens in one bag — vs. only 6 Legendary in the whole general supply. The reference gives rarity counts only, not the individual token identities.

p. 6

Sec 2 The Three Stages

What happens in Stage 1 (Legend Loadout)?

Kit out your squad — all players act simultaneously (no turn order):

1. Draw a Loot Pool: 4 Weapon cards + 4 Common + 4 Rare tokens.  2. Freely trade weapons/tokens with squadmates (not enemies).  3. Equip exactly 2 weapons and set their Ammo.  4. Place loot into Gear/Backpack slots.  5. Everything left over is removed from the game — so pass it or lose it.

pp. 12–13

What happens in Stage 2 (Map Drop)?

Prep: place 1 Reward token + 1 Epic Loot token next to each of the 4 Entrance-board edges; each Legend draws 3 Feats.

Phase A — place at Entrance board (Blue first, alternating): put each Legend at an edge and take that edge's Epic token (keep/drop — a drop here is removed from the game).

Phase B — enter the Map (Red first — Red has initiative): place each Legend outside the matching Map edge and resolve (do not take) the edge's Reward token. Cleanup: remove leftover Epics + all Reward tokens.

Reward tokens: Move 4 · Move 2 + Rare Loot · Move 3 + Common Loot · Move 3 + 1 Ammo. Always resolve the Move first; your first space must be on the Map.

pp. 13–14

How does Stage 3 (Final Encounter) work?

The main fight — up to 8 rounds. Each round: Red turn → Blue turn → Transition (Red always first; initiative never changes). Each turn = (1) resolve Initiative-marker effects, top→bottom; (2) activate each Legend once (2 AP each); (3) advance the marker.

Win immediately by either: (a) eliminating every enemy Legend at least once, or (b) holding 2 VP (2v2) / 4 VP (3v3) at the start of a twice-eliminated enemy's activation. VP come from eliminating enemies (their Deathbox flips to your VP side).

Transition phase: Ring damage to those outside the ring → each draws 1 Feat → tick cooldowns left → advance Round marker & resolve it → flip Draft cards → advance Initiative. Round-8 tiebreak: most VP; then most Health among Legends still holding both Deathboxes; else stalemate.

pp. 8, 14–17

What can a Legend do with its 2 AP?

Basic actions (1 AP each; repeatable unless noted): Move 2 · Interact + Move 1 · Shoot (once per activation only) · Use + Move 1 · Melee + Move 1 · Destroy 2 + Move 1 · Respawn (if eliminated — must be your first action).

Plus: card actions (Free / 1 / 2 AP) · Rush (discard a Feat for Move 1 or +1 MP; once/activation, no AP) · free drop/pick-up of tokens before or after any action. Outside your own activation you may only play REACTION and INSTANT cards.

pp. 15–16

Sec 3 Weapons & Attachments

What is the gold ▲/▼ chevron next to Damage / Aim cards?

A Hop-Up indicator (weapon-card callout #11) — the gun is a factory-modified variant of its base model. ▲ = that stat is better than the base; ▼ = worse. It's purely informational (the printed stat already includes the change); it just explains why a number looks unusually high or low. A Hop-Up may also add a yellow PASSIVE effect.

Different from the ▲/▼ on attachment tokens — the Hop-Up chevrons are baked into the weapon card itself.

pp. 26–27, 45

Do some weapons come with preinstalled attachments?

Yes — Printed Attachments (callout #10), found on the 32 Fully Kitted Weapon cards. They're always active and can't be removed, transferred, or discarded. You can place a matching Attachment token on top, which deactivates the printed one until you remove the token (then it reactivates).

Distinct from a Hop-Up — and a single gun can have both.

pp. 4, 27

What does ENHANCE—SHOOT do on a weapon?

An optional boost you layer onto a Shoot, played during the Aim step (1d). It costs no extra AP (it rides on the Shoot you're already making). The effect is whatever the card says — e.g., +damage per Hit.

Limit: only 1 Enhance—Shoot per Shoot — and Instants that "Play as Enhance—Shoot" count toward that cap. Usable only during your own activation. Unlike a PASSIVE (always on), this is a deliberate one-per-shot choice.

pp. 28, 34, 40

How do Optics affect weapon range?

They don't change the weapon's range. They lower To-Hit Difficulty when the target is within the optic's own range band. Two separate concepts: the weapon's Optimal Range (unchanged; +10 To-Hit per space outside it) vs. the optic's band (where its bonus applies). Modifiers are cumulative, so an optic can offset an out-of-range penalty but never removes it.

Example — C.A.R. SMG at range 2: +10 (one below optimal) −5 (optic covers 1–2) = To-Hit 55.

Sec 4 Gear & Survival

If you equip a blue (Rare) Body Shield in Stage 1, do you start at 30 shield?

Yes — 30/30, but via two effects working together: equipping sets your limit to 30, and the Rare shield's equip bonus restores 10 (20→30), topping you off. It then flips to Exhausted.

The gotcha: "equip = start at the limit" is not the rule — the restore is what fills you. From a 20 start:

Shield equippedLimitRestoreResult
Rare (blue)30+1030 / 30 (full)
Epic (purple)40+1030 / 40 — not 40!
Legendary (gold)40+2040 / 40 (full)
pp. 30, 41

What does the Helmet do?

Protects against Headshots only — never regular Hits or damage. It negates the headshot bonus on Hit cards whose raw Aim value (ignore board modifiers) is at or below its threshold, up to a card limit:

RarityEffect
CommonNo effect
RareIgnore 1 headshot on a card of value ≤ 50
EpicIgnore up to 2 headshots on cards ≤ 60
LegendaryUp to 2 ≤ 60, plus SPECIAL ACTION: 1 AP Interact & Use

Higher-value cards punch through the threshold; extra qualifying headshots beyond the card limit also get through.

pp. 29, 41

Can you have more than 40 Health?

No. 40 is always the Health limit — a permanent hard cap. Any excess healing is lost (Syringe, Medical Kit, Phoenix Kit all cap at 40).

Contrast with Shield, whose limit does vary by Body Shield (20 / 30 / 40 / 40) — that's the only side of your HP pool that can grow. An Epic-shielded Legend tops out at 40 Shield + 40 Health = 80 total, but the Health half is locked at 40.

pp. 30–31, 42

How does the Knockdown Shield work?

Your anti-elimination safety net. It triggers automatically, and only when damage would drop you to 0 Health: it prevents up to its value, applies any remaining damage, then flips to Exhausted.

RarityEffect
CommonNo effect
RarePrevent up to 10 damage
EpicPrevent up to 15
LegendaryPrevent up to 15 + reduce Ring damage to 8

If the overkill exceeds what it can prevent, you're still eliminated. Refresh it by getting eliminated (the reset re-activates it) or by equipping a fresh one — no consumable recharges it.

pp. 30–31, 41

Sec 5 Movement & Positioning

Is a (full) Wall an obstacle?

Yes — Walls are OBSTACLEs (+1 MP to cross, same level or one level up). But a full Wall is also a BLOCKAGE (blocks Line of Sight) and a LEVEL component — unlike a Half-Wall, which is only an Obstacle + Cover.

Level caveat: you can't cross a Wall that sits a level above you (that's the cross-levels rule, not the obstacle tag). And crossing downward across an obstacle is free.

pp. 18–19, 23

What is cover — and does it cost MP to cross?

Cover (COVER: Half-Walls, Respawn Beacons, Supply Bins) makes you harder to hit but does not block LoS. You're "behind cover" when the LoS crosses a cover object (or a connected intersection with cover) and you are adjacent to it. Then any Aim card showing the Cover Auto-Miss icon automatically misses.

Cover itself costs no MP — the OBSTACLE tag is what charges +1. Those objects just happen to be Obstacles (Half-Walls, Bins) or Impassable (Beacons), so crossing them costs for that reason.

pp. 23, 29

What happens if you're pushed off the map?

You can't be — the Map edge is a hard boundary, so it's a Collision. You stop at the edge (don't leave), take Collision damage (4 from a Melee Attack, or the amount on the card causing the Push), and the Push ends immediately.

No ring-out elimination like the video game — you simply bonk the border. (That damage is normal damage, so it could still finish you via Life & Death, but "going off the map" never kills you on its own.)

pp. 18, 33

How do you push diagonally?

The push direction isn't a free choice — you shove the target straight away from you, along the line drawn from your space through theirs. If they're on a diagonal-adjacent space, that line is diagonal → a diagonal push, one diagonal space at a time, in that same direction only (no bending mid-push).

Each step still checks for Collision; on a diagonal, watch the "intersection of connected components" trigger — a diagonal move crosses that corner point.

pp. 18, 20, 33

Sec 6 Grenades & Abilities

Does IMPASSABLE block grenades?

No — IMPASSABLE is a movement-only tag (it stops Legends from entering a space). Grenade landing ignores all components and can even land on an impassable object's space; grenade AoE is limited by BLOCKAGE (Walls) and levels — not by Impassable.

So a Respawn Beacon (Impassable + Cover, but not a Blockage) blocks neither the throw nor the blast. AERIAL grenades (e.g., Rolling Thunder) ignore even Blockages and levels.

pp. 18, 31–32

Do Thermite grenades persist / leave lingering fire?

No. Like all grenades, Thermite is single-use: throw → scatter (draw 1 Aim card) → deal 15 damage to Legends in the AoE → token removed. The video game's lingering wall of fire is not modeled.

The only things that stay on the Map are ability miniatures tagged PERSISTENT — grenades carry no such tag.

pp. 32, 42

What if a Legend is dead when their ability triggers?

It still triggers. Timed abilities (DELAY / DURATION) resolve via the Resolve Initiative-Marker Effects step based on the token's position — not by the Legend performing an action — so elimination doesn't stop them. Elimination removes only the Legend's own mini, and cooldowns keep ticking while they're dead.

The one thing that cancels a pending ability is Destroying its miniature (which sends the token straight to cooldown) — and that's not the same as killing the owner. Caveat: this is inferred from the timing rules, not stated verbatim; check the Legend's info sheet for special wording.

pp. 15, 31, 34, 38

# PART 1 — RULEBOOK

## What is Grimcoven? [RB p.3]

Grimcoven is a ruthless boss battler with RPG elements. Each game is based on one of many
**Scenarios** chosen from the available pool. Each Scenario offers a different world,
different rules, different threats — but the goal always remains the same: defeat the
**Griefbound**, survive, and don't yield to the tempting power of **Lament**.

- In every game you face a terrifying entity — a **Griefbound** — supported by an
  **Elite Enemy** and endless hordes of **Minions**.
- Each round begins with a **dice roll**; the results are your resources. You spend them to
  activate Actions and trigger abilities.
- You collect **Lament** by battling enemies and exploring **Rifts**. Lament grants power but
  every drop corrupts you, bringing you closer to the point of no return.
- Lament lets you develop abilities and buy new Action cards and dice, but it also tears away
  your humanity. You cannot hold back, as enemies also grow stronger with each passing round.
- The battlefield includes diverse **Terrain**, **Portals** connecting different parts of the Map,
  and **Treasures**.

## Winning or losing [RB p.3]

> Hunt the Griefbound. Or die trying.

The game ends when either the Hunters kill the Griefbound, or one of the loss conditions is met:

- **The Hunters run out of time** — an Event card has to be drawn while the Event deck is empty.
- **One of the Hunters dies** (see [RB p.25]).

Additionally, each Griefbound may add new win or loss conditions to the Scenario.

After each Scenario, depending on whether the Hunters win or lose, an appropriate **Epilogue**
should be read.

### Game modes (summary) [RB p.3]

Two main game modes in terms of length:

- **Long Hunt** — estimated 2–4h. Plays through the early, mid and late stages of the Hunt.
  The standard setup instructions describe this mode.
- **Rapid Hunt** — estimated 1–2h. Hunters and the Griefbound begin in the midgame with the
  situation already heated up. Requires a few modifications after the Long Hunt setup, and is a
  bit more complex at the beginning.

Easy mode, Hard mode and Rapid Hunt options are all described on [RB p.30].

## Game setup [RB p.6-10]

> **Open and Play:** for the first game, use the *Tutorial Scenario* booklet. [RB p.6]

### Choose a Scenario [RB p.6]

Take the **Hunt Sheet** and choose a Griefbound. Each Griefbound has 5 available Scenarios, the
first being the easiest and the fifth the most difficult. Choose or randomly pick one Scenario.
Take the **Hunt Book** for that Griefbound and go to the chosen Scenario setup.

The Hunt Book contains the special rules for the Griefbound, Map layouts and component lists for
each of that Griefbound's Scenarios, plus additional rules not present in the setup procedure below.

The rulebook setup below does not list the components used — that list is in the Hunt Book.
Resolve the first setup with the Rulebook as a guide and the Hunt Book as a component list.

### Map setup [RB p.6]

1. **Map tiles** — construct the Map using the codes on the tiles and the diagram in the Hunt Book.
   Most Maps have an additional special 3-space Environment tile added later in setup; it is
   usually random, marked with a question mark.
2. **Lament Rifts** — place Rift tokens randomly face up in marked spaces as shown in the Hunt Book.
3. **Portals** — place a Portal Model in each space with a Portal symbol as shown in the Hunt Book.
4. **Lament** — place `[Lament]` on the Map depending on the number of Players:
   - **2 Players or solo:** 1 Lament in each even-numbered Lament Rift.
   - **3 Players:** 1 Lament in each even-numbered Lament Rift, and 1 Lament in Rift number 7.
   - **4 Players:** 1 Lament in each even-numbered Lament Rift, and 1 Lament in Rifts 1 and 7.
   - **5 Players:** 1 Lament in each Lament Rift.
5. **Treasures** — place 1 random Treasure token face down in each space marked with a Treasure
   symbol as shown in the Hunt Book.

### Environment setup [RB p.7]

Take the Environment deck and draw the cards listed in the Hunt Book. Environment cards come in
three categories:

6. **Terrain** — take a random card for each Terrain type present in the Scenario, reveal them and
   follow the setup instructions. These cards are placed next to the Map and affect the whole
   Terrain they correspond with.
7. **Landmark** — draw 1 card for each marked space on the Scenario Map, reveal them and follow
   their setup instructions. This mostly means placing a corresponding Model (or token) in the
   marked space. Then put the Landmark card next to the Map, close to the Model/token it mentions.
8. **Area** — draw 1 card and add a new 3-space Map tile to the Map. Usually you are then instructed
   to discard the card. Some Scenarios ask for a specific tile, marked with a code.

### Enemies setup — Events [RB p.8]

1a. Take all **Event cards** from the box.
2a. Find the 3 **Griefbound-specific Events** (marked with their name and art on the front).
3a. Add **random Event cards** (of other Griefbounds not present in the Scenario) according to the
    Hunt Book. Put the remaining Event cards back in the box — they will not be used this Scenario.
4a. Shuffle the created **Event deck** and place it next to the Map.
5a. Place the *Event Step I* standee near the Event deck.

### Enemies setup — Minions [RB p.8]

1b. Take 1 **Minion card** according to the Hunt Book and place it face up near the Map. Put the rest
    back in the box.
    *Note: there are 2 types of Minions: Convicted and Undead. The Undead are more complex and difficult.*
2b. Take 8 **Minion Models** matching the Minion type and place them next to the Minion card. Each
    Minion type (Convicted or Undead) uses 2 similar but different Models. Put remaining Models back.
3b. **Spawn the starting Minions:** take 2 Minion Models for each Hunter in the game (in a 5-player
    game only 8 Minions are spawned). Starting from Lament Rift #1 and proceeding in ascending order,
    place 1 Minion Model on each Lament Rift until you run out of Models.
4b. Place the *Minion Step II* standee near the Minion card.

### Enemies setup — Elite [RB p.8]

1c. Take an **Elite Enemy card** according to the Hunt Book. Some Scenarios might ask you to use a
    specific Elite. Place its card near the Map.
2c. Place the matching **Elite Model** on the Map. Some Scenario setups might instruct you to put
    the Elite card face down with a `[Universal Marker]` on the card; in such cases the Elite Enemy
    is not on the Map at the beginning and will arrive later.
3c. Set the L-shaped **Elite level token** below the card. **Elite level = `[#H]` minus 1** — Level 1
    in a solo and 2-player game, Level 2 in a 3-player game, and so on.
4c. Place the *Elite Step III* standee near the Elite card.

### The Griefbound setup [RB p.9]

1d. Place the **Griefbound board** near the Map.
2d. Place the **Griefbound stat card** (with the Scenario number shown on the back) in the matching slot.
3d. Set the **Griefbound Level token** in the bottom part of the Griefbound board. **Level = number of
    Hunters minus 1** — Level 1 in a solo and 2-player game, Level 2 in a 3-player game, and so on.
4d. Take all cards from behind the chosen **Griefbound divider**.
5d. Take all **Stage cards** for this Griefbound. Shuffle them to create the **Stage deck** and place
    it face down in the matching slot on the Griefbound board.
6d. Take the **Final Attack** card listed in the Hunt Book and place it face down in the **Attack deck
    slot**. Put all other Final Attack cards back in the box.
7d. Take the **Opportunity card** listed in the Hunt Book and place it face up in the **This Turn slot**.
    Put all other Opportunity cards back in the box.
8d. Create the Griefbound **Attack deck**: take the number of random cards of each Tier according to the
    table in the Hunt Book, based on the number of Hunters. Put unused cards back in the box.
    *Note: cards are divided into three Tiers, marked with the number of dots on the back of the card.*
9d. Shuffle the Griefbound Attack deck and place it face down **on top of the Final Attack card** in the
    Attack deck slot.
10d. **Fill the Griefbound Attack track:** place the top card of the Attack deck face up in the *This Turn*
     slot, covering the Opportunity card. Then place another card from the top of the Attack deck face up
     in the *Next Turn* slot.
11d. Follow any Griefbound-specific setup instructions in the Hunt Book.
12d. Place the *Griefbound Step IV* standee near the *This Turn* slot on the Griefbound board.
13d. Place the *Time Track Step V* standee in the Start space of the **Time track** on the Griefbound stat card.
14d. Place the **Griefbound Model** on the Map as shown in the Hunt Book.

### Hunter setup [RB p.10]

Each Player:

1e. Takes one **Hunter board** in their preferred color and places it in front of themselves.
2e. Chooses one Hunter and takes their **Hunter tile**, any special components, and all Item and
    Evolution cards from behind their divider (see Hunter descriptions on [RB p.26]).
3e. Places the Hunter tile in the center of the Hunter board.
4e. Sets their `[Health]` dial to match their maximum `[Health]`. **The Player with the least maximum
    Health takes the First Player token.**
5e. Places the **Assist marker** in their Hunter's board color in the Assist slot on the Hunter board.
6e. Takes one **Corruption marker** and places it in the 0 slot on the **Corruption track**.
7e. Shuffles the Evolution deck and places it face down near their Hunter board.
8e. Takes both **Help cards** (Game Round Order and Basic Actions) and places them near their play area.
9e. Chooses starting Items — one **Weapon** and one **Armor** card. Returns the rest of the Item cards
    to the box. Places the chosen cards face up near the Hunter board and gains the **dice** listed in
    the Starting Dice section of each of those cards.
10e. Places their **Hunter Model** in a chosen starting space on the Map according to the Hunt Book.
11e. Performs any additional Hunter-specific setup (see Hunter Specific rules on [RB p.26]).

### Final steps [RB p.10]

1. Put all remaining dice and tokens near the Map.
2. Take the **Corruption deck**, shuffle it and place it face down next to the Map.
3. Take the **Secret deck**, **DO NOT shuffle it**, and place it next to the Map.
4. You may now include the rules for Solo mode, Rapid Hunt, Hard mode or Easy mode — see [RB p.30].

**Prologue** — read this after finishing setup; it contains a lore fragment connected to the
Griefbound and Scenario. Each Player rolls their dice. Start the game by proceeding to the Hunter Phase.

## Round overview [RB p.11]

> **Component limits.** The components are limited. If there are not enough components, do **not** use
> replacements! In most cases simply skip the part of the effect that regards it. **In the case of
> Status tokens and Hunger tokens, Hunters take damage instead of gaining the missing tokens.** [RB p.11]

> **Rules on the cards and components may overwrite the basic rules contained in the Rulebook.** [RB p.11]

> **`[#H]`** represents the number of Hunters present in the game and should be read as a number
> (e.g. 3 in a 3-player game). It is often used to scale the magnitude of effects and therefore
> difficulty, depending on the number of Hunters. [RB p.11]

Each round has a set structure, split into 4 Phases:

1. **Hunter Phase** — Hunters play their Turns by resolving Actions: moving on the Map, attacking,
   defeating Enemies and ultimately killing the Griefbound to win the Scenario.
2. **Enemy Phase** — the Enemies pay the Hunters back. First Events are resolved, then all Enemies
   activate (Minion, Elite and Griefbound activations).
3. **Lament Phase** — each Hunter resolves the effects of Corruption and Hunger, and may spend
   Lament to upgrade their Hunter.
4. **Refresh Phase** — Hunters Refresh all Actions, Regain dice, resolve the effects of Statuses and
   prepare for the upcoming round.

### Game round order (Help card summary) [RB p.5]

**1. HUNTER PHASE** — Hunter Turn: perform 2 Actions OR Pass. All Hunters play their Turns until all
Hunters Pass.

**2. ENEMY PHASE**
- I. *Event step:* Check Critical Injuries. Resolve all Permanent Events. Draw and resolve a new Event card.
- II. *Minions step:* Activate all Minions.
- III. *Elite step:* Activate all Elite Enemies.
- IV. *Griefbound step:* Resolve the Attack card in the This Turn slot. Discard the Attack card and Refill the track.
- V. *Time track step:* Move the Time track standee and resolve its effect.

**3. LAMENT PHASE**
- I. *Corruption step:* Draw and resolve a Corruption card.
- II. *Spending Lament:* `[Lament]`: Advance Corruption track by 1. `[Lament]`: Discard 1 Hunger token.
  `[Lament]`: During Evolution, redraw Evolution cards.
  When advancing into `[Evolution]` resolve Evolution: draw 2 Evolution cards and keep 1 or discard both.
  If you've reached 15 Corruption, flip your Hunter tile to the Corrupted side.

**4. REFRESH PHASE**
- I. *Refresh step:* Pass the First Player token. Refresh all Actions and Regain all dice. Resolve all Refresh effects.
- II. *Status step:* Each Hunter may discard any number of `[Tactic]` to discard 1 Status token for each discarded.

## Hunters Phase [RB p.11]

### Hunter Turns [RB p.11]

Hunter Turns are resolved in **clockwise order, starting with the First Player**. Each Hunter performs
their **Turn** by resolving **exactly 2 Actions**. Then the next Hunter in clockwise order resolves
their Turn.

**Hunters may play as many Turns as they like (2 Actions per Turn) as long as they have available dice
and/or Actions.**

If a Hunter cannot resolve any more Actions, or chooses not to, they have to **Pass**. After a Pass, the
Hunter discards all remaining dice from the Active pool and will not be able to resolve any more Actions
during this Phase — they are skipped in the Turn order. The Hunters Phase continues until all Hunters
Pass. **The last Player who has not Passed yet may play as many consecutive Turns as they wish until
they Pass.**

### Hunter dice [RB p.11]

Players use 6-sided **Hunter dice** as their main resource, in 4 different colors, each representing an
area of expertise:

- `[black die]` — offensive capabilities. Most common symbol: `[dagger]`.
- `[green die]` — speed and dexterity. Most common symbol: `[arrow]`.
- `[blue die]` — defensive abilities. Most common symbol: `[shield]`.
- `[purple die]` — arcane prowess. Most common symbol: `[star]`.

### Dice slots [RB p.11]

To use an Action, Players must place a die into a slot with a matching symbol. Some Actions require
multiple slots to be activated. Possible slots are:

- **Blank/any slot** — may be activated by a die with **any** symbol.
- **Single-symbol slot** (`[dagger]`, `[shield]`, `[star]`, `[arrow]`) — a die with an appropriate symbol
  has to be used. **A die with a double symbol may also be used, but the second symbol on it is "lost"** —
  the Hunter does not get the surplus back in any way.
- **Double-symbol slot** — either a die with a double symbol, **or two dice with single symbols** matching
  the slot, have to be used. When using 2 dice, stack them on top of each other in this one slot.

Some Actions may have **alternative costs**. You may use one or the other to activate the Action.

**Regardless of the slots used, the Action cannot be used again until Refreshed.**

> Example: an Action may be activated by using 2 `[dagger]` symbols (on a single die or two dice) **or**
> `[arrow]` and `[star]` symbols combined. No matter which cost was used, the other cannot be paid until
> the Action is Refreshed. [RB p.11]

### Refreshing Actions and Restoring dice [RB p.11]

Dice may be in any of 3 different places during the game:

- **Active pool** — dice available to be used for Actions.
- **Dice in Action slots** — marking that the Action was used.
- **Discarded pool** — unavailable dice.

Flow: Active pool → (Use Action) → Dice on Actions → (**Refresh Action**) → Discarded pool →
(**Restore Die**) → Active pool. Dice also go from the Active pool to the Discarded pool via
**Discard Die**.

### Spending dice [RB p.12]

At the beginning of the round, Players have access to all of their dice in their Active pool. Then the
dice are either placed on Action slots to activate their effects (going to dice spent on Actions) or are
discarded to resolve some effects (going to the Discarded pool).

**Refreshing Actions** — dice on Actions may be discarded by using a Refresh Action effect and go to the
Discarded pool. You can also Refresh Actions with Universal Markers to discard them. This way the Action
may be used again.

**Restoring discarded `[any die]`** — discarded dice may be put back into the Active pool by using a
Restore `[any die]` effect, in which case they are **rerolled** and placed in the Active pool.

### Resolving Actions and Action Types [RB p.12]

Actions can be found in various places: on a Hunter board and Hunter tile, on Evolution cards, Item cards,
Map tiles, Environment cards and others.

**When resolving an Action, it has to be resolved fully before another is made.** Effects of this type of
Action are resolved **from left to right and from top to bottom**. If an Action or an effect states
"discard X to …", discarding the listed component is considered a **necessary cost** of resolving the effect.

The most common type of Action is the one marked with **dice slots**. To use such an Action, a Hunter
places a die (or multiple) into the available slots with matching symbols above. After placing dice, the
effect of an Action is resolved. **For Actions, you can always use multiple dice to meet the symbol
requirements.**

Other Actions are free to use — a Player simply declares they are using them. They may be marked in two ways:

**Action with a Universal Marker slot** — this type of Action is free to use, but **only once per round**.
When used, mark it with a Universal Marker `[Universal Marker]` and resolve the effect. The marker is taken
from the common pool. This type of Action may be Refreshed — in such cases discard the marker, after which
the Action might be used again.

**Actions with an 'Action:' descriptor** — e.g. *"**Action:** discard `[dagger]` and `[arrow]` to Restore 1
discarded `[any die]`."* This type of Action is free to use and most likely found on Secret cards or
Environment cards. While the Action itself is free, it may have a cost inside the effect.

### Basic Actions and the Assist Action [RB p.12]

The same for each Hunter (printed on the Hunter board):

| Action | Slot | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Run** | any symbol | Move your Hunter Model up to a number of spaces equal to your Hunter Movement value `[Move]`. |
| **Sprint** | `[arrow]` | Move your Hunter Model by up to `[Move]` **+1** spaces. During this Action you may Move through **Portals** — a Hunter on a space with a Portal may Move to another space with a Portal as if it was adjacent. |
| **Tactic** | any symbol | Gain 1 Tactic token and place it on any of your empty Tactic slots. You may choose to immediately use the Tactic token to reroll any number of `[any die]` in your Active pool. |
| **Cleanse** | `[star]` | Discard up to 2 chosen Status tokens from your Hunter board. |
| **Assist** | any symbol | Give your Assist marker to the chosen Hunter. That Hunter places the Assist marker in their Active dice pool. |

**Assist markers** may be used instead of a die. An Assist marker represents an exact same die with the same
exact symbol as **the die placed on the Assist slot** on the Hunter board of the Player whose Assist marker
is being used. [RB p.12]

> ### Movement golden rules [RB p.12]
>
> - You may only Move to **adjacent** spaces (the ones physically connected to your current space).
> - When you **end your Action, including Movement**, on a space with a Lament token or a Treasure, you
>   **may** gain it. If there is a **Status token** on that space, you **have to** gain it. Hunters do not
>   gain tokens if they are Moving *through* a space without ending the Action there.
> - Each space can contain up to **4 Models**, with the **Griefbound counting as 2 models**. You may Move
>   *through* a fully occupied space, but when you **end** your Movement there, you have to **push one other
>   Model to an adjacent space** (see spaces, [RB p.23]).

## Dealing Damage [RB p.13]

Many Actions allow the Hunters to deal Damage `[Wound]`, which is crucial to defeat Enemies including the
Griefbound. **When a Hunter resolves an effect that deals `[Wound]`, it may only target an Enemy and never
a Hunter, unless specifically stated otherwise.**

When an effect allows you to deal `[Wound]`, place that number of tokens on the appropriate component in
the game. (Damage tokens come in values 1, 3 and 5.)

Effects describe the **Range** in which the `[Wound]` can be dealt. The first number of the Range icon
shows the **minimum** Range and the second the **maximum** Range (e.g. `Range 0-2` allows the Hunter to
target an Enemy on their space or up to 2 spaces away).

Effects may require having the target in **Line of Sight (LoS)** — a straight line unobstructed by Obstacle
spaces or Fog (see Map on [RB p.23]).

### "On a kill" [RB p.13]

The **On a kill** keyword is commonly found in many effects. The instruction following this keyword is
triggered after an Enemy has been killed. It may be specific to a type of Enemy:

- **On a Minion kill:** resolved after a Minion is dealt any `[Wound]` and is removed from the Map.
- **On an Elite Enemy kill:** resolved after an Elite Enemy is dealt `[Wound]` equal to or exceeding its `[Health]`.
- **On a Griefbound card kill:** resolved when a Griefbound Attack card is dealt `[Wound]` equal to or
  exceeding its `[Health]`. This results in **removing** the card from the Attack track, which is different
  from **discarding** it! (see [RB p.19])

## Defense and Tactic tokens [RB p.13]

There are two types of tokens universal for all Hunters — **Defense tokens** (value `[Def 2]` or `[Def 4]`)
and **Tactic tokens**. Each has to be stored in a matching Tactic or Defense slot — **maximum one token per
slot**. If there are no slots available, the Hunter cannot gain the token.

**Defense tokens** allow Hunters to prevent `[Wound]`. When a Hunter is being attacked they may decide to
discard a Defense token to reduce the incoming `[Wound]` by the value of the Defense token.
*Note: when defending you do not gain the excessive value back!*

When a Hunter gains a `[Def 2]` token while already having one, they **may add them together and gain a
`[Def 4]` token** (putting both `[Def 2]` tokens back into the common pool).

> **Example [RB p.13]:** Mark uses a "Gain `[Def 2]`" Action. He already has a `[Def 2]` token and only one
> Defense slot, so he exchanges two `[Def 2]` tokens for one `[Def 4]` token. Later in the round, Mark is
> attacked by a Minion which deals 2 `[Wound]`. Mark decides to discard his `[Def 4]` token to reduce the
> incoming damage. Since Defense tokens cannot be split, he does not gain a `[Def 2]` back, even though the
> damage was blocked with a surplus.

**Tactic tokens** allow the Hunter to reroll dice in their Active pool. A Hunter may discard a Tactic token
to reroll any number of dice currently present in their Active pool. This may be done either:

- At any moment, without spending an Action.
- As an Action during the Hunter's Turn in order to stall some time.

## Enemy Phase [RB p.14]

> ### Enemies golden rules [RB p.14]
>
> - Enemies can only have `[Def 2]` tokens on themselves and always use their Defense tokens when being attacked.
> - When an Enemy Moves, they **Move towards the closest Hunter**, unless stated otherwise.
>   1. If there is more than one closest Hunter, Move towards the Hunter with the **highest Corruption**.
>   2. In case of a tie, **Players decide**.
>   3. If there are multiple ways the Enemy may Move — **Players decide**.
> - When Enemies enter fully occupied spaces they **swap with a Model in this order: Companion, other
>   Minion, Elite, Hunter.**

### Enemy attacks [RB p.14]

Enemy attacks mostly take one of two forms: attacking with **Enemy dice**, or dealing a **set amount** of
`[Wound]` and/or Statuses.

For dice attacks, sum up the results from all rolled Enemy dice used in this attack and resolve them — by
taking the listed amount of `[Wound]` and/or gaining Statuses.

You may discard Defense tokens to prevent `[Wound]` by the number indicated on the discarded token. **The
surplus of Defense is not gained back! Statuses cannot be blocked with Defense tokens in any way.**

Each time an Enemy deals `[Wound]` to your Hunter, mark it on the `[Health]` dial on your Hunter board by
subtracting the appropriate amount.

**In case of any confusing effects, always apply `[Wound]` and Statuses before resolving any other effects.**

**Enemy die faces (12 sides):** 7 × `[claw]`, 4 × `[sun]`, 1 × `[scythe]`. [RB p.14]

### Event step (Step I) [RB p.14]

At the beginning of the Enemy Phase, Hunters have to **check if they have Critical Injury cards** (see
[RB p.25]) and apply their effects. Then Hunters resolve Event card effects in order:

1. All **Permanent Event** effects (if present).
2. **Draw a new Event.**
3. Place new `[Lament]` on the Map (optional).
4. Resolve the **main effect** of the Event.

**If the Event deck is empty and a new card must be drawn, the Griefbound escapes and the Scenario is lost.**

### Events [RB p.14]

Events are drawn and resolved at the beginning of each Enemy Phase. They bring unexpected and mostly
negative changes to the table.

**Permanent Events** — most often drawn from Griefbound Stage cards and marked with a *"Permanent Event"*
tag. They are **not discarded** unless specified, so their effect may be applied during multiple rounds.
In the rare situation where you have more than one Permanent Event, place them next to each other to keep
track of which was first; **when resolving multiple Permanent Events, start with the one drawn earlier**.

**Event effects** are divided into two parts:
- The **first part is general** and is always resolved.
- The **second part is conditional** and resolved only if the Griefbound being hunted in this Scenario
  matches the name on the card.

Event effects often contain a choice or an optional effect. **If the effect states "discard X to …" or
"gain X to …", the first part of the effect is mandatory in order to resolve the second.**

## Minions [RB p.15]

### Minion step (Step II) [RB p.15]

Activate **each Minion Model on the Map** by resolving one activation from the Minion card. **Start with
the Model closest to any Hunter.**

Minions are the weakest type of Enemies, although they should not be underestimated.

All Minion types share some similarities: [RB p.15]

1. **All Minions have 1 `[Health]`.** A single hit is enough to kill them.
2. **Killing a Minion rewards the Hunter who killed it with 1 Lament `[Lament]`.**
3. Each Minion can hold up to one `[Def 2]` token — in the dedicated holder on its Model or standee.
4. Each Minion type has **2 activation options** — each Minion Model acts according to the conditions
   listed next to said actions. **During activation each Minion Model resolves only one of the listed
   activations.**
5. Minions have a **Dice Effect** section on their card — when this Minion attacks and rolls Enemy dice,
   check the result in this section.
6. Minions of the **Undead** type have a **passive ability** written in the top part of their card.

### Spawning Minions [RB p.15]

When any effect instructs you to Spawn a number of Minions (usually tied to `[#H]` — most commonly due to
the Time track or at the beginning of the game), take the listed number of Models and place them on the Map
as follows:

1. **Count the number of Minions on the Map.**
2. Find the Lament Rift with the number **1 higher** than the number of Minions on the Map and Spawn the
   first Minion there.
3. Continue placing Minions in the Lament Rifts in **ascending order** until there are no more Models available.

Components are limited — **if there are not enough Minions in the reserve, Spawn as many as possible.**

If the Lament Rift is **fully occupied** and a Minion has to be Spawned, it **pushes one other Model to an
adjacent space**, as if entering this space via Movement. In an extreme case, when the space is full of
Models that cannot be pushed (Environment Models or the Griefbound), **the Minion is not Spawned**.

> **Example [RB p.15]:** Players have to Spawn 4 Minions (2×`[#H]` in a 2-player game). They take 4 Models
> from the reserve. They count the number of Minions currently on the Map — which is 3. They place one
> Minion in each Lament Rift numbered 4, 5, 6 and 7 in ascending order.

## Elite Enemies [RB p.16]

### Elite step (Step III) [RB p.16]

Activate **each Elite Model on the Map**, starting with the one **closest to any Hunter**. Resolve one of
the activations present on the Elite Enemy depending on the conditions stated on the card. Repeat for every
Elite Model on the Map.

Elite Enemies might not be on the Map due to the starting conditions of the Scenario, or because they were
killed. In such cases, **discard 1 `[Universal Marker]` from each Elite card that is face down. When the
last marker is discarded this Elite is spawned, but does not activate instantly — it will act in the next
Enemy Phase.**

### Elite Enemy characteristics [RB p.16]

1. Elites have a varying number of `[Health]` depending on the Elite itself and the **Elite Level token**.
2. Killing the Elite grants the Lament indicated in the bottom central part of its card. **Two different
   numbers show how much Lament the killer gets and how much any other Hunter gains.**
3. Elites have multiple Actions and will only use **one** during each activation, similar to Minions.
4. The **Dice Effects** section shows what happens when the Elite rolls symbols during an attack.
5. Each Elite Enemy may hold up to one `[Def 2]` token on its card.

Each Elite Enemy type has **3 variants**, represented by 3 different cards. **There can be only one Elite
of its type in the game at any given time.** One Elite in each type is significantly stronger than the rest
and is called a **Named Elite**. Not recommended for first games — they tend to be significantly more difficult.

**List of Named Elite Enemies:** Alicia the Twisted, Thristan the first Vampire, Croakholm, Hilhorn's Shadow.

### Spawning an Elite [RB p.16]

In some cases an Elite Enemy is not present on the Map at the beginning of the Scenario, or a new Elite
joins the fray during the Hunt. **When an effect instructs you to draw a random Elite card, ignore all cards
from the Elite types already present in the game!**

When instructed to Spawn an Elite Enemy:

1. Place the Elite Enemy card next to the Elite Step III standee.
2. Set up an Elite Level token — it should be of **equal Level to the Griefbound's**.
3. Find the **Portal space closest to the Griefbound** and place the corresponding Elite Enemy Model there.
   **Do NOT activate this Elite Enemy afterwards.**

If there are no Portals on the Map, or all Portal spaces are fully occupied, the Elite Enemy cannot be
Spawned. Keep the card face down and place one `[Universal Marker]` on the Elite Enemy card. The Elite is
not gone, just waiting for a better moment to rejoin the fight.

### Killing an Elite [RB p.17]

When an Elite Enemy has `[Wound]` tokens on its card in number **equal to or exceeding** its `[Health]`,
resolve the following:

1. All Hunters gain Lament as a reward.
2. Discard all tokens from the Elite card.
3. Discard the Elite Model from the Map.
4. Flip the Elite Enemy card face down. The active Player **rolls an Enemy die** and checks the result on
   the back of the Elite card — this shows if you managed to truly kill it or just chase it away for some time.

**All Elite Enemies with their cards face down are considered "not on the Map" for all effects that require it.**

> **Example [RB p.17]:** The Alchemist is dealt the 6th `[Wound]` which is equal to its health. The Model is
> discarded from the Map, the Elite Enemy card flipped, and the active Player rolls an Enemy die to determine
> the outcome.

## The Griefbound (Boss) [RB p.18]

### Griefbound board [RB p.18]

The Griefbound board holds most of the information about the Griefbound. Unlike other Enemies the Griefbound
uses many components: Griefbound Stat cards, Attack cards, Final Attack cards, Opportunity cards, Stage
cards, and other special components.

**In order to defeat the Griefbound and win the Scenario, the Hunters have to kill enough Attack cards,
proceed to the Final Act, and kill the Final Attack card.**

Board slots: Griefbound Stat card slot; Griefbound Stage deck slot; slot for the Griefbound Final Attack
and Attack deck; slot for the Opportunity card and *This Turn* Attack card.

### Griefbound Stat card [RB p.18]

Each Scenario uses a different Griefbound Stat card. It holds:

1. Its name and art.
2. Scenario number (on the back).
3. Special effect.
4. Defense slots (the Griefbound may hold one `[Def 2]` in each slot).
5. Dice Effects.
6. Time track.

### Griefbound Attack step (Step IV) [RB p.17]

Activate the Griefbound Attack card in the *This Turn* slot by resolving **all effects from the Griefbound
Attack card in order from top to bottom**. When the Griefbound attacks and rolls Enemy dice, **sum up the
effects from the Stat card along with the Dice Effects modifiers from the Attack card**.

After resolving the card, perform the following:

1. **Discard** the Griefbound Attack card from the *This Turn* slot. Place the card on the discard pile to
   the right of the slot. If there are any tokens on the card, discard them.
2. Move the card from the *Next Turn* slot (if present) to the *This Turn* slot; **all tokens on the card
   (including `[Wound]`) remain for the next round**.
3. **Refill the Attack track:** draw new cards from the Attack deck and place them face up in empty slots.
4. If the **Final Attack card is visible** in the Attack deck slot, reshuffle the Attack cards from the
   discard pile and place them in the Attack deck slot **to cover** the Final Attack card. Continue refilling
   the Attack track.

**If there are not enough cards in the discard pile to refill the Attack track and cover the Final Attack
card, then the Hunt enters its Final Act!** (see Attack track on [RB p.19] and Final Act on [RB p.20])

> **FOCUSED** — resolve those effects **unless there are `[#H]` `[Wound]` on this card**. [RB p.17]

> **Example [RB p.17]:** The *Cursed Shot* Attack card has 3 bullet points to resolve. The Griefbound will,
> in order: Move 2 spaces toward the closest Hunter; then Move 2 spaces toward the closest Hunter so that it
> has a Hunter in its Line of Sight (only if the Griefbound is FOCUSED); lastly it will attack all Hunters in
> Range 0-5 and Line of Sight with 3 dice. The effects of the attack are present on the Stat card, but the
> `[claw]` result additionally deals 1 Curse.

### Attack cards [RB p.19]

One of the most important components of the Griefbound is the Attack cards — they not only specify how it
will act but also **track how much health the Griefbound has left**. Most cards are unique, but all share:

- Title.
- Attack Actions.
- Dice Effects modifiers (optional).
- **Body part and health of the card** (combined with the Griefbound Level token below).
- **Lament reward** (first number for the Hunter who killed the card, the second gained by each other Hunter).

### Killing the Attack cards [RB p.19]

To remove the Griefbound's Attack card, Hunters have to deal enough `[Wound]` to it. When a card has an
amount of `[Wound]` **equal to or greater than its `[Health]`** (shown on the Griefbound Level token by the
Body part icon at the bottom of the card), resolve the following:

1. **All Hunters gain Lament** as a reward — the first number indicates how much the killer gets, the second
   how much every other Hunter gains.
2. The Attack card is placed **under the Hunter board of the Player who dealt the last `[Wound]` as a
   Trophy**. It will not come back into the Attack deck during this Scenario.
3. If it was a card from the *This Turn* slot, **the killer resolves the uncovered Opportunity card by
   rolling an Enemy die**.

### Attack track, Attack deck and the discard pile [RB p.19]

The three slots in the bottom part of the Griefbound board are the **Attack track** — it holds Attack cards,
an Opportunity card and the Final Attack card. The first slot from the left is reserved for the Final Attack
card and the **Attack deck**. The two remaining slots on the right store the Attack cards that will be
resolved in the current and the next round. Underneath the Attack card in the *This Turn* slot there is an
**Opportunity card** — resolved when the Attack card on top of it is **killed** and removed from the game.

**Important distinction between discarding and killing an Attack card:**

- **Discarding** — after resolving an Attack card in the Enemy phase, the card from the *This Turn* slot is
  discarded, meaning placing it in the discard pile to the right of the Attack track. **The card will
  probably come back into the deck at some point.**
- **Killing** — removing it from the Attack track and placing it under the Hunter board of the Player who
  killed it as a **Trophy**. **It will not be seen again during the game.**

### Opportunity cards [RB p.19]

When a card from the *This Turn* slot is **killed** (removed), Hunters uncover the Opportunity card lying
beneath. **The Hunter who dealt the finishing blow rolls an Enemy die** to determine what effect occurs —
this will most likely be a bonus for the Hunters in close range of the Griefbound and an instruction to
refill the Attack track. **Range and LoS of effects present on the Opportunity card are drawn from the
Griefbound.**

Sometimes the result may **stagger** the Griefbound — the Attack track will not be refilled during the
Hunters Phase instantly. In such cases the Griefbound will do nothing in the Enemy Phase as there is no
Attack card in the *This Turn* slot. The track will be refilled at the end of *Griefbound Step IV*, so the
Griefbound's weakness is only temporary.

### Refilling the Attack track [RB p.19]

This always happens at the end of *Griefbound Step IV* of the Enemy Phase, after resolving the Griefbound
Attack, or most times after resolving an effect from an Opportunity card. Resolve in order:

- Slide the Attack card from the *Next Turn* slot (if present) to the *This Turn* slot.
- Draw new cards from the Attack deck and place them in the *This Turn* and *Next Turn* slots, if they are
  still empty.

If at any point of this process the **Final Attack is uncovered**, check if the Final Act is triggered.

### Entering the Final Act [RB p.20]

**The Final Act can only be triggered when enough Attack cards have been killed.**

Entering the Final Act is checked **every time the Attack deck is depleted**. In such cases, check if there
are **2 or fewer Attack cards total** left in the game; this includes Attack track slots **and the discard
pile**. If so, trigger the Final Act. Otherwise, refill the Attack track and continue playing as normal.

When the Final Act is triggered:

- Remove any remaining Attack cards from the Attack track and discard pile.
- Remove all remaining **Secret 2 cards (Critical Injury)** from the Secret deck.
- Reveal the Final Attack card and place it in the *This Turn* slot.
- **Level up the Griefbound and each Elite Enemy.**
- If this happens during the Hunters Phase, the Griefbound **instantly ends the Hunters Phase** — proceed to
  the Enemy Phase.

**The Final Attack card cannot be revealed in any other way.**

### Final Act and killing the Griefbound [RB p.20]

During the Final Act, the rules change significantly:

- **The death of a single Hunter no longer ends the Scenario** — Hunters fight to the last man standing!
- Killing the Final Attack card is different from other Attack cards.

When the Final Attack card is dealt enough `[Wound]`, instead of removing it, the Hunter **rolls an Enemy
die** to determine if the Griefbound has been defeated:

- On a **`[scythe]`** result, the **Griefbound is dead and the Scenario is won!**
- On any other result, **all `[Wound]` is removed and the Griefbound's Level is lowered** — check the Final
  Act section on the Griefbound board for details. **If the Griefbound's Level cannot be lowered further,
  the Scenario is won.** Otherwise, the game continues.

### Stage cards [RB p.20]

Stage cards represent the Griefbound growing in power during the Hunt, whether by anger or excess of Lament
present on the battlefield. **Each time the Time track enters a space with *Stage*, draw and resolve a new
Stage card from the top of the deck.** Those cards may contain all types of power ups for the Griefbound,
ranging from an alternative Special effect to a new Lose condition for the Hunters.

One common thing Stage cards do is **leveling up the Griefbound and Elites** — this instructs you to change
the current Level token of the Griefbound and all Elite Enemies to the one with a higher number.

### Griefbound additional rules [RB p.20]

Each Griefbound has some unique mechanics and components — mostly present in the Special effect on its
Griefbound tile or Attack cards. For detailed information on how each Griefbound special mechanic works, see
the **last page of the corresponding Hunt Book**.

### Time track step (Step V) [RB p.20]

Move the Time track Step V standee **one space forward** on the Griefbound's Time track (following the
arrows) and resolve the effect of the space the standee is **entering**. There are three main types of
spaces on the Time track:

- **Spawn:** Spawn 2×`[#H]` Minions (see Spawning Minions on [RB p.15]).
- **Stage:** Draw and resolve a new Stage card.
- **Special:** Resolve the section titled *Special* on the Griefbound Stat tile.

## Lament Phase [RB p.21]

During the Lament Phase, Hunters absorb the energy of the Lament they have gathered so far, and possibly
suffer the consequences of abusing the power that comes with it.

### Corruption step [RB p.21]

Starting from the First Player and continuing clockwise, **each Hunter draws and resolves 1 Corruption card**.

- The **top part** of the card refers to **Hunger** — if the Hunter has no Hunger, it should be skipped.
- The **bottom part** checks the **Corruption level** of the Hunter — the higher the Corruption, the worse
  the effects triggered. **The Hunter resolves only one effect that matches their Corruption.**
- After the card is resolved, place it on the **bottom** of the Corruption deck.
- Some cards instruct you to **reshuffle the deck**. This happens **after each Hunter resolves their
  Corruption card**.

### Corruption [RB p.21]

The Corruption track measures how much the Hunter has abused the Lament — the higher the Corruption, the
worse the effects. Each round, every Hunter will draw and resolve 1 Corruption card to check how their mind
and body are withstanding the effects of the Lament.

**When a Hunter reaches a Corruption level of 15, they turn to their Corrupted form** — the most powerful
the Hunter can become.

**Losing Corruption** — some effects allow Hunters to lose accumulated Lament Corruption. Simply move the
Corruption marker backwards by the number of spaces mentioned (e.g. "Lose 2 Corruption" means moving the
marker 2 spaces backwards).

- When losing Corruption, **Hunters do not lose or gain any Evolutions** (ignore `[Evolution]` icons).
- **Reducing Corruption below 15 does not flip the Hunter back to the non-Corrupted side.**
- However, when **re-entering** an `[Evolution]` in the Lament Phase by spending Lament, **a Hunter may
  evolve again!** This may happen any number of times, as long as the Hunter is able to lose Corruption in
  opportune moments.

### Final Perdition [RB p.21]

The Final Perdition is a state in which the Hunter loses themselves by becoming **the Pretender** — a
half-realized, but nonetheless dangerous Griefbound.

All rules regarding the Final Perdition are present on one of the **Secret cards**. Brief notes:

- The Final Perdition has more chances to trigger once a Hunter is high on the Corruption track.
- The Final Perdition may shorten or prolong the game once triggered.
- A Hunter who falls into the Final Perdition will become a **Pretender** — an additional Griefbound to be
  Hunted. Once the Hunter unlocks their Final Perdition, the slot on the Hunt sheet next to the Pretender
  Scenario will be marked.

### Evolution step [RB p.21]

Divided into two parts: **Lament Spending** and **Additional Corruption**.

**Spending Lament** — each Hunter may spend their Lament `[Lament]` **one token at a time** to evolve or
discard Hunger. This step may be done **simultaneously**. Any number of times each Hunter may:

- Spend 1 `[Lament]` to **advance their Corruption track by 1**.
- Spend 1 `[Lament]` to **discard 1 Hunger**.

Each time the Corruption marker advances to a space with an `[Evolution]` icon, **resolve an Evolution**.
When the Corruption marker reaches the space marked with number **15**, flip your Hunter tile to the
**Corrupted side** and resolve any immediate effects.

**Each Lament token remaining on the Hunter boards after this is discarded!**

**Evolution** — each time a Hunter resolves an Evolution:

1. Draw the top 2 cards of the Evolution deck.
2. Any number of times you may spend 1 `[Lament]` to **redraw** the Evolution cards — put the current ones
   on the bottom of the deck and draw 2 new cards.
3. You may choose to **keep one** of the Evolution cards. **If you choose not to keep any of them, gain 1
   `[any die]` in a chosen color.** All Evolution cards not chosen during an Evolution are placed at the
   bottom of the Hunter's Evolution deck.

**Additional Corruption** — after spending and discarding all the gathered Lament, the Hunter may decide to
succumb to the Corruption. In such situations, the Player may **once** advance their Corruption marker to
the next `[Evolution]` on the Corruption track **without resolving it**.

## Refresh Phase [RB p.22]

### Refresh step [RB p.22]

Each Hunter prepares their dice for the upcoming round. Resolve in order:

1. Pass the **First Player token** to the next Player in clockwise order.
2. Return all **Environment cards** used last round to the appropriate place.
3. Return all **Assist tokens** to their owners.
4. Each Hunter discards all `[Universal Marker]` from their cards.
5. Each Hunter gathers all their dice from each occupied Action slot and their discarded dice pool, then
   **rolls all their dice** and places them into their Active dice pool.

### Status step [RB p.22]

Hunters may spend `[star]` to discard Status tokens **before** resolving their negative effects.

1. Each Hunter may discard any number of magic `[star]` symbols to discard that many Status tokens (**Hunger
   is not a Status**). If a Hunter uses dice with `[star]` symbols, they place the dice in the discarded dice
   pool near their Hunter board.
2. Resolve all relevant Status effects and proceed to the Hunters Phase.

### Statuses and Hunger [RB p.22]

Each time a Hunter gains or is dealt a Burn, Stun, Curse or Hunger, they place the corresponding token on
their Hunter board.

**Statuses — can be discarded using a Cleanse Basic Action:**

| Status | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Burn** | In the Status step, take **1 unblockable `[Wound]` for each Burn** you have. **Burns are not discarded afterwards.** |
| **Stun** | When you gain a Stun token, immediately place it **on the text of a chosen Action**. Using a Stunned Action **has no effect**. **Stuns are not discarded when the Stunned Action is used!** Some effects may instruct you to Stun a specific Action, in which case place the Stun token on the mentioned Action, if possible. |
| **Curse** | When you gain any number of Curses, draw **one** random **Secret 1** card (each Hunter may have only one Curse effect card at any given time). The Secret card specifies the Curse effect and will most likely take into account how many Curse tokens you have. **When you discard your last Curse token, return the Secret card to the Secret deck.** |

**Hunger is NOT a Status, and therefore cannot be discarded by the Cleanse Action!** Hunger does nothing on
its own — the top part of the Corruption card specifies the effect of Hunger. If you don't have any Hunger,
ignore the top part of the Corruption card entirely.

Additional notes: [RB p.22]

- **Enemies cannot gain Status tokens or Hunger** unless an effect clearly states otherwise and explains how
  this Status affects said Enemy.
- Status tokens on the Map are collected by a Hunter who **ends their Movement (or Action in general) on a
  space with a Status token**.
- **Components are limited.** If a Hunter has to gain a Status or Hunger token but the pool is empty, **they
  take 1 `[Wound]` for each token they cannot gain!**

## Other rules — Map [RB p.23]

### Spaces [RB p.23]

A **space** is a single hexagonal area of the Map. **Each space may hold up to 4 Models.** This includes
Hunters, Enemies and Environmental Models such as Portals.

When a Model **ends their Movement** on a fully occupied space (either by Moving or Teleporting), **push one
of the Models on that space to an adjacent space.**

Hunters may choose which Model to push. **Enemies have to push Models in this order** (if present on the space):

1. Companions.
2. Minions.
3. Elite Enemies.
4. Hunters.

**The Griefbound or Environment Models can never be pushed** when a Model enters its space. The Griefbound
Model **counts as 2 Models**, so when entering a fully occupied space it pushes **2 Models** out of the space
to accommodate its size and respect the Model limit.

### Range, Line of Sight (LoS) [RB p.23]

Range is measured in spaces and indicated by the Range icon, which shows the minimum and maximum range of an
effect. E.g. `Range 0-1` means the effect can be resolved on the same or an adjacent space; `Range 2-2`
allows the effect to target only a space exactly 2 spaces away.

**Line of Sight (LoS)** is a straight, unobscured line of spaces. To be in a Line of Sight means to be on one
of the spaces in a clear line from another. **Only Obstacles and Fog spaces block LoS.** Some effects require
both Range and LoS. **Edges of the Map are considered Obstacles.**

> *Note: A Model is always in its own Line of Sight. Models on the same space are in one another's Line of
> Sight.* [RB p.23]

### Terrain and special spaces [RB p.23]

Some Map tiles share the same color — all spaces in the same color are called a **Terrain** (Forest, Swamp or
Cemetery). These have additional effects or Actions during each Scenario. The Map is usually created by
combining **2 different Terrains**.

Other special spaces:

| Space | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Deep Water** | Models **cannot enter** a Deep Water space, indicated by the `[No Entry]` icon. **Range and LoS might still be measured through it.** |
| **Fog** | Obscured visibility. **LoS cannot be measured through the borders of this space**, indicated by the `[Fog]` icon. **Two Models on the same Fog space are still in one another's LoS.** |
| **Obstacles** | This space **cannot be entered** by any Model, Hunter or Enemy. **Range and LoS cannot be measured through it.** Indicated by both `[No Entry]` and `[Fog]` icons. |
| **Lament Rifts** | If there is a Rift token on a space, that space is considered a Lament Rift of a specific number. It **does not overwrite the Terrain** it is on and has **no effect on its own**, but many effects (Spawning Minions, adding Lament on the Map, etc.) reference those spaces. |

### Treasures, Lament and Status tokens on the Map [RB p.23]

General rules for tokens placed on the Map: **Enemies do not interact with them** (unless said otherwise);
Hunters **may pick up** the positive ones (Treasures, Lament, etc.) and **have to pick up** the negative ones
(Statuses, Hunger, etc.) if they **end their Action** on a space with said tokens. Most of these tokens when
picked up are gained.

**Treasure tokens** — a positive token containing random rewards, **one of which to choose from is always a
Heal**. When a Hunter decides to pick up a Treasure token, they reveal it, read the available options and
choose one to resolve. After that the token is **removed from the game**. If a Treasure token is being added
to the Map, it should be pulled randomly from the still-available pool (the ones not removed yet).

## Environment [RB p.24]

Various points of interest are encased under Environment, divided into 3 groups:

**Terrain Environment** — a card placed next to the Map, showing the additional effects of Actions that can
be used on a whole Terrain (all spaces in the matching color), even if the Terrain is divided into several
smaller parts by other Terrains or special spaces. These cards usually contain a *"Once per Hunters Phase"*
Action available for the Hunters as long as they are on a Terrain space matching the card color. Once used,
the *"Once per Hunters Phase"* Action will not be available until the Refresh Phase. During setup one random
card for each Terrain is drawn and placed next to the Map.

**Landmark Environment** — a point of interest in the form of a Model or token standing on the Map,
accompanied by a card describing what Actions or effects are present on the space with the Model. During
setup usually between 2 and 3 such cards are drawn and corresponding Models or tokens placed on the Map in
marked spaces. Similarly to Terrains you may often find a *"Once per Hunters Phase"* Action on those cards,
but in those cases they are mostly **no-cost** Actions. In exchange, they may come with an alternative cost
in Lament or Health, or contain a risk of a dice roll.

**Area Environment** — a special, **3-space tile** added to the Map. It contains Actions and possible story
elements. Actions themselves are mostly free, but will likely guide you to the Secret deck with possible
surprises. During setup usually one such tile is added to the Map by drawing a random Area Environment card
and placing the corresponding tile in the marked place on the game Map.

## Critical Injury and Hunter Death [RB p.25]

When your Hunter's `[Health]` is reduced to **0**, resolve the following:

1. **Heal 1**, discard all Status and Hunger tokens.
2. **Draw and read a Secret card 2.**

As long as you have a **Secret card 2 (Critical Injury)** next to your Hunter board, **you are ignored by all
Enemies and negative game effects.** This includes all effects dealing `[Wound]`, Statuses, Hunger, Enemy
targeting, Enemy attacks, Corruption deck effects etc.

The Critical Injury card contains a **quest** to be fulfilled. **If this quest is not completed before the
beginning of the next Event step, the Hunter dies and the Scenario is lost.** If the quest is completed, the
Hunter will Heal and **remove the Critical Injury card from the game** — it cannot be drawn again during the
current game.

**If a Hunter drops to 0 `[Health]` and there are no more Secret 2 cards to draw, the Hunter is dead and the
Scenario is instantly lost.** This changes during the Final Act (see [RB p.20]).

## Secrets [RB p.25]

Some random effects, adventures, or otherwise hidden things are gathered in the **Secret deck**. Whenever an
effect instructs you to draw and resolve a Secret card, find the appropriate number on the reverse of the
card, and resolve the card.

Notable, commonly used cards in the Secret deck:
- **Curse Effect** cards — **Secret 1**, five different.
- **Critical Injury** cards — **Secret 2**, three.

They are always on the **top of the deck** for convenience, since Players might draw them multiple times
during a single Hunt.

There may be multiple Secret cards with the same number — in such cases, **randomly pick one of them** to
resolve. Secret cards 1 come in five different copies containing the Curse effects; each time you are
instructed to draw Secret 1, you may find a different Curse!

Do not browse through the Secret deck — it may spoil the fun. **Note that the numbering is not always
consecutive within the Secret deck.**

## Companions [RB p.25]

Some game effects may allow you to gain **Companions** — special cards accompanied by an additional Model to
aid you during the Hunt. Companions grant additional Actions and effects for Hunters. When you gain a
Companion:

- Draw 1 random Companion card.
- Place the card next to your Hunter board.
- Find the Companion Model and place it on your space.

**Companions are not targeted by Enemies and are the first Models to be pushed out of a space due to the
Model limit.**

## End of a Scenario [RB p.25]

After the end of the Scenario, read the appropriate **Epilogue** in the Hunt Book. If the Scenario is won,
Players should mark it on the **Hunt Sheet** to track their progress.

When Scenarios numbered **5** for each Griefbound are marked as won, Players may read the Epilogue of the
whole game, found on [RB p.34].

## Hunters [RB p.26]

### Hunter board anatomy [RB p.26]

The Hunter board tracks: current Corruption, current `[Health]`, Defense slot, Tactic slot, Basic Actions.

Labeled parts: Corruption Track (with **Evolution spaces**), Basic Actions, Assist special action slot,
Tactic token slot, Health dial, Defence token slot. **When you reach 15 Corruption, flip your Hunter Tile to
the Corrupted side.**

### Hunter tile and special mechanics [RB p.26]

Each Hunter is represented by a **Hunter tile** with Hunter-specific information: maximum `[Health]`,
Movement value `[Move]`, special abilities and/or Actions. **A Hunter tile has two sides: non-Corrupted and
Corrupted.**

### Item and Evolution cards [RB p.26]

Each Hunter has their own **Item deck** (Weapons and Armor cards) and **Evolution deck**.

- **Item cards** are chosen at the beginning of the game during setup. They provide additional Actions and
  determine the starting dice.
- The **Evolution deck** is used when the Hunter resolves an Evolution for spending Lament. It contains many
  powerful Actions and effects crucial for completing the hunt.

## Hunter specific rules [RB p.27-29]

### Duality [RB p.27] — Complexity: Medium

**Playstyle.** Duality plays a role of either agile short-range damage-dealer, tank or healer depending on
the starting gear. Keep in mind how you use your resources: mainly Duality-exclusive Light and Dark tokens
and Defense tokens. Being one token short may disrupt your turn heavily, so gain those tokens whenever the
game allows you to — mostly with Duality's special ability, **Balance**: for using a `[black die]`-only
Action gain 1 Light, and for using a `[blue die]`-only Action gain 1 Dark.

**Special mechanic: *Light and Dark*.** Duality uses two types of tokens to boost her Actions — **Dark**
tokens are earned for using Attack dice `[black die]` and **Light** are earned by using Shield dice
`[blue die]`. **There is a limit of 3 for each token.** If Duality would gain a 4th token of any kind, she
cannot. Duality is the only Hunter who has easy access to a Dice Restore Action, which costs **both** Light
and Dark tokens. Gaining the Light and Dark tokens due to the Balance ability on Duality's Hunter tile is
resolved **at the beginning of resolving an Action**.

**Additional Setup.** Place 3 Light and 3 Dark tokens next to your Hunter board. You do not have any
available at the beginning and may gain them later in the Scenario.

**Duality as Deputy (Solo Mode).** No additional rules or changes.

### Gunslinger [RB p.27] — Complexity: Low

**Playstyle.** Long-range single-target attacker or short-to-medium-range crowd control. Can output lots of
damage but often needs some time to set up. **Most effects and attacks of the Gunslinger require LoS to hit
the target**, so positioning is key.

**Special mechanic: *Bullets* and *Weapon Range*.** The Gunslinger has access to **Bullet tokens**, stored in
Bullet slots. **Each Bullet slot can store only one Bullet token**, and defines what additional effect an
attack may have. When the Gunslinger uses an Action from his Weapon or Evolution card that deals `[Wound]`,
he may decide to boost this attack with **ALL** Bullet effects he has available (**he cannot choose to use
only some of them**). **Bullet tokens are discarded after being used.**

This is the only Hunter for which the choice of the Starting Weapon matters for later attacks found on
Evolution cards. Each Starting Weapon has **"Weapon Range"** written at the bottom of the card. Evolution
cards may use this Weapon Range instead of a set value.

**Additional Setup.** Take all Bullet tokens and place them near your Hunter board. You do not have any
available at the beginning of the Scenario.

**Gunslinger as Deputy (Solo Mode).** Bullet tokens are not placed in Bullet slots, but placed on the Deputy
card instead. They still might be used when resolving an Action that deals `[Wound]` to deal more. Evolution
cards add more passive effects to Actions when using Bullets.

### Keeper [RB p.28] — Complexity: Low-Medium

**Playstyle.** Frontline damage-dealer or tank. One of the few Hunters with access to multitarget attacks,
big self-heal Actions, or reversing the effects of Hunger or Corruption. The Keeper has a **lower than
average Movement value**, so before a few evolutions or having `[green die]` available for Sprint they may
feel sluggish.

**Special mechanic: *Craving*.** During the game the Keeper awakens his demonic sword, represented by
**Craving tokens**. The more Craving the Keeper has, the stronger his Hunter tile Action becomes. He may also
**spend Craving from the sword to use some Actions without using dice**, which allows him to drastically
increase the amount of attacks or Moves done in a single round.

- **Gaining** Craving allows him to receive one Craving token.
- **Spending** Craving means discarding a Craving token to resolve an effect.
- Some of the Keeper's Actions may spend Craving as an **additional or alternative cost**.

**Additional Setup.** Take **5 Craving tokens** and put them next to your Hunter board to form the Sword.

**Keeper as Deputy (Solo Mode).** No additional rules or changes.

### Technomancer [RB p.28] — Complexity: Medium-High

**Playstyle.** Medium-range attacker or support. Her abilities may allow her to discard or exchange Status
tokens, Move Enemies or allies around the Map, or interact with Portals. She can be quite fragile, or exert
herself with excess Burn or Stun tokens, heavily hindering her abilities.

**Special mechanic: *Technology die*.** The Technomancer has access to unique **orange Technology dice**. She
starts the game with one of these dice and in the course of the game may unlock the second one. **These dice
cannot be gained in any other way — a game effect has to specifically state to gain `[tech die]`!**

These dice give access to Technomancer-specific Actions on her Item and Evolution cards. Notable Actions of
this type are **Weapon and Armor power ups**, which activate **ALL** effects on the Evolution cards with a
matching cog layout color. The Technomancer may have multiple power up Evolution cards in the same color
attached to her Weapon or Armor. Evolution cards with an **orange/blue cog layout** are considered to have
Weapon/Armor Actions matching the color.

**Technology die rundown (6 faces):** 4 × Cog, 1 × Double Magic `[star][star]`, 1 × Double Agility
`[arrow][arrow]`. [RB p.28]

**Additional Setup.** No additional setup.

**Technomancer as Deputy (Solo Mode).** At the beginning of the game, gain 1 `[tech die]` (included on the
Deputy card). **Slots with a Cog may only be activated by using the `[tech die]`. The `[tech die]` may be
used to activate Action slots with no symbol on them.**

### The Mannequin [RB p.29] — Complexity: High

**Playstyle.** Fits a plethora of roles: support, long-range damage-dealer, tank or disruption. Due to the
Personality tokens it works very differently round to round. **Limited early game potential**, but gives
other Hunters access to **String Actions** that in clutch moments may help reposition or squeeze an
additional point of missing `[Wound]`.

**Special mechanic: *Personalities, Arms and Strings*.**

Each round the Mannequin chooses one **Personality** to guide its body, in the form of a Personality token.
**In each Refresh step the Player chooses one Personality token.** The Personality can be used during the
Hunters Phase. **The same Personality cannot be chosen twice in a row.** Personality tokens come with a
downside of **blocking one of the Basic Actions**.

The four Personality tokens: [RB p.29]

| Personality | Blocks | Action | Activates |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commander** (blue) | Block your Cleanse Action. | Gain 1 Stun. Flip this token. | Activate all Commander bonuses. |
| **Messenger** (green) | Block your Tactic Action. | Gain 1 Curse. Flip this token. | Activate all Messenger bonuses. |
| **Mage** (purple) | Block your Sprint Action. | Gain 1 Hunger. Flip this token. | Activate all Mage bonuses. |
| **Soldier** (orange) | Block your Move Action. | Gain 1 Burn. Flip this token. | Activate all Soldier bonuses. |

**The Soldier Personality token is unique because it allows the Player to choose which of the Run or Sprint
Actions is blocked.** Personality tokens can be chosen even if the Action blocked by it has Stun. The token
gives access to special Actions, allowing you to resolve all bonus effects for this Personality. Bonus
effects are found on Arm and Evolution cards in the frame in the color of a Personality. **These effects may
be resolved in any order chosen by the Player.**

The Mannequin has access to unique **double-sided Item cards called Arms** (with **Strings** on the back).
During setup they choose **4 Arms** to start and deal **2** of the remaining Arms as Strings to other
Hunters. Arms might be used by the **Bloodless Husk** ability to reduce incoming `[Wound]`, while Strings
allow the Mannequin or other Hunters to resolve additional Actions with the Mannequin.

**Additional setup.** The Mannequin does not have standard Item cards. Instead choose one of two Arms for
each Personality (marked with color) and gain the corresponding dice. Flip the unchosen Arms to the String
side and give 2 chosen String cards to other Players. Put the remaining 2 String cards back in the box.
Take all Personality tokens and place them next to your Hunter board. Choose one of them for the first round
of the game and block the appropriate Action.

**Mannequin as Deputy (Solo Mode).** As deputy, the Mannequin gives **2 random String cards** to the main
Hunter at the beginning of the Hunt.

### Death Jester [RB p.29] — Complexity: Medium-High

**Playstyle.** Quick damage-dealer or support. Actions are often high-risk-high-reward, allowing outstanding
displays of power but risking her Action not resolving at all. Very fragile — a single miscalculation can
result in a Critical Injury or death.

**Special mechanic: *Fate cards and Gambling*.**

The **Fate deck** holds the Fate cards used in Gambling effects. **In each Refresh step the Player draws Fate
cards to their hand. There is no limit to how many cards the Player can hold at a time.** Cards in hand may
be used later when Gambling.

The Fate deck consists of **14 cards**, 5 figures (Knight, Princess, Prince, Queen, and King) distributed
between **3 basic suits (Violet, Yellow, and Red)**. **The Hanged Man card is considered a fourth suit. The
Jester card, while in sequence, can be used as any figure in any suit.**

**Gambling:** if an Action has a **Gamble frame**, you may decide to perform it — **after placing dice but
before resolving the Action**. To do so, put the **top three cards** from the Fate deck in the sequence in
front of you. Then you may **replace any number of Fate cards from the sequence with ones in your hand**.
Replaced cards are discarded. **Meeting the required rank is considered a success; not meeting it is a
failure. On a failed Gamble, the Action has no effect.**

All cards used during a Gamble are discarded and placed next to the discard dice. **The discarded cards are
reshuffled back into the Fate deck after a Pass, or when the deck is empty and new cards have to be drawn.**

**List of possible ranks:** [RB p.29]

| Rank | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Pair** | Two figures of the same kind |
| **Army** | Three Knights |
| **Successors** | A Prince and a Princess |
| **Rulers** | A King and a Queen |
| **Color** | All cards in the same suit |

**The Fate deck (14 cards):** [RB p.29]
- 1× Hanged Man (Black)
- 1× Jester (White)
- 1× King (Violet)
- 1× Queen (Yellow)
- 2× Princess (1 Yellow, 1 Red)
- 2× Prince (1 Red, 1 Violet)
- 6× Knight (2 Red, 2 Violet, 2 Yellow)

**Additional setup.** Shuffle the Fate deck and place it face down next to your Hunter board. Draw 1 Fate card.

**Death Jester as Deputy (Solo Mode).** No additional rules or changes.

## Game modes [RB p.30]

**All modes and difficulty modifiers are compatible with one another.** Players may include as many as they
like in their game. Even all of them.

### Solo mode [RB p.30]

When playing solo, your Hunter receives support in the form of their **Deputy** — one of the Hunters you
didn't choose. **A Deputy is considered a Hunter for all game effects.**

**Deputy setup:**

1. Take one Hunter board, and place the **Deputy Action tile** to cover the Basic Actions section.
2. Choose one Hunter. Take that Hunter's **Deputy card** and place it **non-Corrupted side up** in the
   appropriate space on a Hunter board.
3. Take **3 Universal Markers** and place them near the Deputy board.
4. Take your Deputy Hunter Evolution deck, shuffle it and place it face down near the Deputy Hunter board.
5. Place the Deputy Model on the Map as instructed in the Scenario setup.

The Deputy resolves their turns like a normal Player, but they use **Universal Markers to activate Actions
instead of dice**. To accommodate this, all Deputy Action slots have no symbol on them. Key points:

- The number of Deputy Actions is limited by the number of their Universal Markers. **In most cases, one
  Universal Marker activates one of the Deputy's Actions.**
- **You cannot place a Universal Marker on an already occupied slot.** Refresh Action and Restore discarded
  `[any die]` effects for the Deputy, respectively: discards a Universal Marker from the already used Action,
  or places a Universal Marker from the Discarded to Active pool.
- The Deputy and the Hunter **may Assist one another**. The Deputy may use an Assist marker on any of their
  Actions. The Hunter may use the Deputy's Assist marker in any Action slot **with no symbol in it**.
- The Deputy's Universal Markers are considered **a die with no symbol on it**. They cannot be used in
  effects that require a specific symbol (e.g. "Discard `[star]` to Heal 1").
- **The Deputy cannot gain dice from rewards and Treasure. Instead they gain `[Universal Marker]`.**
- In the Refresh step, take all the Deputy's Universal Markers from each Action slot and place them near the
  Deputy's Hunter board.
- **Evolution step:** the Deputy may spend Lament similarly to other Hunters — to discard Hunger or advance
  the Corruption track. **Deputy Evolution is a choice between gaining 1 additional Universal Marker or
  drawing the top card of the Deputy Evolution deck** and placing it next to the Deputy's Hunter board.
  **The Deputy cannot use Lament to redraw Evolution cards.**

> **Remember:** The Deputy is considered a Hunter for each game effect, including `[#H]` count and Critical
> Injury. **If the Deputy dies the Scenario is lost.** [RB p.30]

### Rapid Hunt [RB p.30]

Plays a single Scenario much faster. Follow the additional setup instructions below, then the Griefbound
specific modification:

- Remove `[#H]` random Attack cards from the Attack deck.
- Resolve **2 Stage cards** (including the Level Ups for the Griefbound and the Elite Enemies).
- Set the Time track to the **last space with Stage** without resolving it.
- Discard all `[Lament]` from the Map.
- Discard the top 3 cards of the Event deck, without resolving them.
- **Each Hunter sets their Corruption to 7.**
- **Each Hunter resolves Evolution 3 times.**
- Resolve any additional Griefbound specific setup.

| Griefbound | Setup modification |
|---|---|
| Undead General | Reveal 2 Banner cards. |
| Swarm Mistress | Place 2 additional Swarm tiles on the Map in `Range 0-1` from any Hunter. |
| Convergence | Each Hunter gains 3 Burns. |
| Tormentor | Each Hunter takes `[Wound]` equal to the Scenario number. |
| Graveyard Dragon | Each Hunter gains 2 Curses. |
| Devourer | Each Hunter gains 2 Hunger. |
| Pretender | Resolve Special twice. The Griefbound gains `[Def 2]` for each Defense slot. |

### Difficulty customization [RB p.30]

Players may include as many Easy or Hard mode modifiers as they like. **Note that some Easy and Hard mode
options contradict each other.**

**Easy mode modifiers** — resolve any number at the beginning of the Scenario:
- Each Hunter resolves 1 Evolution.
- Each Hunter gains `[Def 2]`.
- Lower the Griefbound and/or Elite Level by 1 (minimum 1).
- Change the Elite Enemy to a non-Named variant.
- Change the Minion to the Convicted type.
- Add up to 2 Random Event cards to the Event deck.
- Perform an additional `[Lament]` setup on the Map.

**Hard mode modifiers** — resolve any number at the beginning of the Scenario:
- Increase the Level of the Griefbound and/or Elite Enemies by 1.
- Change the Elite to a Named variant.
- Change the Minion to the Undead type.
- Discard the top card of the Event deck.

## Pretenders [RB p.31]

Pretenders are **additional Griefbounds to be hunted**. The Pretender Scenarios can be unlocked by achieving
the **Final Perdition** with the corresponding Hunter.

When you decide to play against a Pretender:

1. Pick a random Scenario for any Griefbound (you may draw a random Griefbound Stat card) and follow the
   Scenario setup in the Hunt Book for that Griefbound.
2. Instead of the components for that Griefbound use the **Pretender's components**. The Pretender Model uses
   the Hunter standee/miniature with the **Griefbound base** to show the size of the Enemy.
3. When constructing the Griefbound's Attack deck use a number of Pretender cards depending on the number of
   Hunters:
   - **Solo and 2 Hunters:** 7 random cards.
   - **3 Hunters:** 8 random cards.
   - **4 Hunters:** 9 random cards.
   - **5 Hunters:** all Attack cards.

**While hunting a Pretender, Players cannot play as the Hunter version of this character.**

### Pretender Duality [RB p.31]

**Special mechanic: *Light and Dark*.** Similar to the Hunter version, Duality uses the Light and Dark tokens
to empower her attacks.

**Keywords:**
- **Gain Light/Dark** — place a Light/Dark token on the Griefbound Stat card.
- **X Light/Dark** — on an Attack card, the Griefbound will resolve an additional effect if she has enough
  tokens available. After that the tokens used are discarded.

### Pretender Gunslinger [RB p.31]

**Special mechanic: *Bullets*.** On his Griefbound Stat tile there is a track to place Bullets in each time a
**Forge** effect is resolved. **Place Bullet tokens from the right to the left** (according to the arrows
printed).

Bullet tokens are used in the **Special effect** to deal more `[Wound]` or increase its range. Like the
Hunter version, **the Gunslinger uses all Bullet tokens or none**, and when used, the tokens are discarded
from the slots.

**Keyword: Forge a Bullet** — place a Bullet token in the **rightmost empty slot** on the Bullet track.

### Pretender Keeper [RB p.31]

**Special mechanic: *Craving*.** The Pretender Keeper collects Craving tokens. **At the beginning of
*Griefbound Step IV*, before resolving the *This Turn* Attack card, if the Keeper has 5 Craving, resolve this
card twice, and discard all Craving.** This effect may trigger **only once per Enemy Phase**.

**In cases when there is no card in the *This Turn* slot, do not discard Craving.**

### Pretender Technomancer [RB p.32]

**Special mechanic: *Technology dice*.** Some effects use `[tech die]` to be upgraded and randomized. Most
notably, the Attack cards may be **Enhanced** by placing `[Universal Marker]` on them. If there is a marker
on an Attack card, it resolves all additional effects marked with the **Enhanced** keyword, additionally
rolls 1 `[tech die]` alongside the Enemy die during attacks and resolves Dice Effects marked with
Cog / `[star]` / `[arrow]` icons.

**Keyword: Enhance** — place `[Universal Marker]` on Attack cards, **one marker per card starting from the
*This Turn* slot**. When a card with a marker resolves an Attack, roll `[tech die]` along with any Enemy die
to see what additional effects occur. **Each Attack card may have only 1 marker.**

### Pretender Mannequin [RB p.32]

**Special mechanic: *Personality tokens* and *Strings*.** The Pretender Mannequin collects **Active
Personalities** on their Stat card. Specific Personalities may boost Attack cards for the Griefbound —
sometimes by having a specific Personality active or by simply having a number of Personalities active.

During the Hunt, Hunters may obtain String cards. They may use them as normal, but **the Pretender Mannequin
is considered an Enemy for the purpose of all those effects.**

**Keyword: Activate random Personality** — take a random Personality token and place it on the Griefbound
Stat card. From now on, it is considered Active.

### Pretender Death Jester [RB p.32]

**Special mechanic: *Fate cards*.** Contrary to the Hunter version, **the Pretender Death Jester gathers all
her revealed Fate cards in a sequence. The sequence is unlimited.** Attack cards or Dice Effects may check if
the sequence contains a specific rank. In such cases, resolve an additional effect, and **at the end of
*Griefbound Step IV* put the cards used in the rank back into the Fate deck and reshuffle it.**

**The only card that has an effect instantly after being revealed is The Hanged Man.** In such a case, follow
the instruction on the Pretender Stat card.

## Keywords [RB p.36]

| Keyword | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Move X** | Model is moved by the indicated number of spaces. This might use `[Move]` as value (Movement rules on [RB p.12-13]). |
| **Sprint** | Resolve the Sprint Action (see Basic Actions [RB p.12]). |
| **Gain `[any die]`** | Take a chosen/specified die from the common supply, **roll it** and add it to your Active pool. |

| **Restore discarded `[any die]`** | **Reroll** a die from the Discarded pool and put it into the Active pool. |
| **On a kill** | An effect triggers when an Enemy is dealt enough `[Wound]` and a component is removed. This might specify a Minion, Elite or Griefbound card kill. |

| **Lament Rift** | Space on the Map with a Lament Rift token. |
| **Pass** | The Hunter will not be able to take any more Turns this round. Usually when no more Actions can be made (see Hunter Turns [RB p.11]). |
| **Teleport** | Place the Teleporting Model in the designated space. **Measuring Range for this effect ignores Obstacles. This counts as Moving into the space.** Teleporting to a fully occupied space pushes one other Model to an adjacent space (Model limit, [RB p.23]). |
| **Spawn** | Place additional Model(s) on the Map (Minions [RB p.15], Elite Enemies [RB p.16]). |

# PART 2 — OFFICIAL GAME FAQ V1.2 (January 2026)

## General questions [FAQ p.1]

**Damage dealt vs Hunger**
**Q:** Most Corruption cards instruct to deal damage and discard Hunger tokens for each damage dealt. If this
damage was blocked with the Defense token, do I still discard Hunger?
**A:** No. The damage blocked by the Defense tokens does not count as dealt. This applies to other effects
such as one of the Actions of Duality's *Assault Armor*. Also, if you have for example 6 Hunger and you have
dealt only 3 due to the fact that the attacked Hunter had only 3 Health left, you discard only 3 Hunger. This
is because you have dealt only 3 damage and not 6.

**Multiple Stun/Curse tokens on a single Action**
**Q:** Can I place multiple Status tokens on a single Action?
**A:** No.

**Space fully occupied by the Griefbound/Environment**
**Q:** There can be a situation in which all 4 model slots on a space are occupied by the Griefbound and
Environment models (e.g. Convergence's Tornadoes). Can a Hunter enter such a space?
**A:** No. This space cannot be entered by any model (even another Environment). **Teleport effects to such a
space cannot be performed. Models can still move through such space, but cannot end their movement there.**

**"Move X" versus "Move up to X"**
**Q:** Is there a difference between "Move X" and "Move up to X"?
**A:** For Hunter's effects — no. A Hunter may always perform a shorter movement if they choose to do so.
Unless it is a forced movement (see below).

**Voluntary vs forced movement**
**Q:** How do I differentiate between "voluntary" and "forced" movement?
**A:** When a Hunter performs a movement as their own Action or an effect coming from a "friendly" source
(other Hunter, Companion, etc.) it is called a **voluntary movement**, and it can be shorter than the effect
suggests (e.g. instead of Move 3, Hunter Moves 1). When a Hunter is moved by an outside "hostile" effect like
an Enemy, Environment, or Hunger, it is a **"forced" movement. It must be resolved to the fullest extent
possible.**

**Enemy movement**
**Q:** If an Enemy has to "Move 3" and it is 2 spaces away from me, does the Enemy resolve a full movement of
3 and moves past me?
**A:** No. The Enemies usually want to get as close to a Hunter as they can, so if they have to Move 3 and the
closest Hunter is 2 spaces away, the Enemy will only Move 2, disregarding the last point of movement. Some
effects may change this rule.

**On enter effect**
**Q:** Does the "on enter" effect happen if I cross a space with such an effect, or only if I end my movement
there?
**A:** The effect happens **only if a model ENDS its movement** on a space with "on enter" effect.

**Q:** Does "on enter" happen on teleport effects? What about the forced movement?
**A:** **Yes, both those effects trigger "on enter".**

**Q:** If I'm already on a space/terrain with an "on enter" effect, does it trigger on any of my Actions
(similar to picking up Treasure and Lament)?
**A:** No.

**Q:** Can "on enter" trigger on itself? E.g. when the River terrain card moves me by 1 space down the river,
does another "on enter" effect happen immediately?
**A:** No.

**Q:** I'm on a Tormentor's Torture Device tile. I'm forced to Move 1 towards the closest Torture device. I do
not move because I'm already on a Torture Device. Do I resolve the "on enter" effect?
**A:** No.

**Refreshing the Assist Action**
**Q:** What happens if I Refresh the die placed in the Assist slot? Do I take the Assist marker back from the
Player?
**A:** No. The Assist marker remains in that Player's possession until the Refresh phase. Until then, the
Assist marker mimics the die placed in your Action slot. In this case, it has nothing to copy, so **the Assist
marker is treated as a "faceless" and "colorless" die.**

**Refreshing the Action with itself**
**Q:** Can an Action Refresh itself? E.g. *Hunger Overflow* has an effect "Refresh 1 Action". Can *Hunger
Overflow* Refresh itself?
**A:** No.

**Target vs Enemy**
**Q:** Some card effects allow the player to include additional "targets". What is the difference between an
Enemy and a "target"?
**A:** Target is a broader classification. The game contains entities that are not Enemies, but still can be
damaged — e.g. Undead General's Banners or Ironclad Safe on one of the Secret cards. While not being Enemies,
those Actions can include them as "additional targets".

**On kill effects and Opportunity cards, order of resolution**
**Q:** If I kill a Griefbound's Attack card, what happens first — the Opportunity or "On a kill" effect from my
Evolution card?
**A:** **Players may decide the order of resolution.**

**Tokens on a card that is being discarded**
**Q:** I'm instructed to discard a card/return a card to the deck, and there are tokens/dice on this card. What
happens to those tokens/dice?
**A:** They are also discarded. The tokens return to the common pool (or their owner if this token was Hunter
specific), and dice are placed in the discarded pool of their owner.

## Corebox Hunters/Griefbound questions [FAQ p.2]

**Death Jester multiple Gambles**
**Q:** How to resolve a card that allows me to "Gamble any number of times"? How does it interact with passive
effects "on a Successful Gamble"?
**A:** After placing dice on the Action but before resolving it, you may Gamble any number of times. You may
decide to stop Gambling after each one. **On a failed Gamble, all successes are lost, the Action has no effect,
and you cannot Gamble further. You still resolve any "on a failed Gamble" effects.** After each successful
Gamble, you may decide to stop, resolve the Gamble effect (usually, repeating an Action X times), and cash out
all "on a successful Gamble" effects (each such effect is resolved X times, where X is the number of successful
Gambles).

**Death Jester Corrupted form "flip the Fate deck"**
**Q:** After the Fate deck is flipped face up, can I look through it or is only the top card visible?
**A:** Only the top card is visible.

**Mannequin String cards**
**Q:** When I flip an Arm card to the String side after the *Bloodless Husk* effect, is this String given to the
chosen Hunter or do I keep it?
**A:** The Mannequin player keeps the card in String form.

**Q:** If I lose/flip an Arm card due to an effect, how can I regain/flip it back?
**A:** By any effect that allows for it. Regaining Arms is very rare — in some form, it is done after reaching a
Corrupted Form (Corruption 15). Flipping the String cards back can be done by a single card in the Evolution deck.

**Technomancer attachment power-ups**
**Q:** Since the "All Weapon/Armor attachment power-ups are now active" Action on Technomancer's Weapon/Armor
card is considered a Weapon/Armor, does it automatically trigger all attachment bonuses of its kind?
**A:** **No. It allows those bonuses to be activated later by other Weapon/Armor Actions.**

## Expansions Hunters/Griefbound questions [FAQ p.2]

**Clockwork Giant status tokens**
**Q:** There are Stun tokens / Curse tokens / dice on a Gear card, and I have to change Gear. What happens to
the tokens?
**A:** The tokens/dice are discarded.
